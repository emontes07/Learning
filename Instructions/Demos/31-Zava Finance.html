Reference

Welcome

Today you are the controller of Granite Gloves, one of eight businesses in Zava Safety (a fictitious division). September just closed. By the end of the session you will have taken one month-end from the raw comparative P&L all the way to your Monthly Exec Summary deck and Business Review slides, then turned the whole process into a reusable skill.

We are not here to turn accountants into AI experts. We are here to remove work you should not have to do manually.

One story, eight chapters

Each chapter builds on the one before. What you find in Chapter 1 becomes a question in Chapter 2, an answer in Chapter 3, a sentence in Chapter 4 and a slide in Chapter 5. Everything you produce lands on a Story sheet in your P&L workbook, so you never lose your thread.

Why the web today?

3. Chat, Plan and Edit

Chat

Questions, explanations and drafts. Nothing in the workbook changes.

Plan

Copilot lists its steps first; you approve. Best for multi-step builds.

Edit

Shown as Allow editing. Copilot changes the workbook. Ctrl+Z undoes.

4. Ground rules

You own the output
Copilot gets you to a strong first draft faster. You are accountable for every number and every word. You can outsource the processing, but not the responsibility.
Using your real files
The same prompts work on your own reports. Keep them in your Bunzl OneDrive or SharePoint and follow Bunzl's data policies.

5. Know your files

  • Comparative P&L (PL tab): rows start at 36. Month columns: F Actual, G Budget, H Var $, I Var %, K Prior Year, L Var vs PY $. YTD columns: O Actual, P Budget, Q Var $, T Prior Year, U Var vs PY $.
  • Two kinds of rows: GL accounts (5300-2530 - TEMPORARY LABOR) and subtotals (Payroll Exp - D&W, Total Sales). Add both and you double count.
  • Story sheet: section 1 rows 6-10, section 2 rows 14-21, section 3 rows 26-33, section 4 rows 37-47, section 5 rows 51-53. The prompts tell you where to write.
  • Forecast file: Op Costs are negative; on the Adjustments tab a positive Op Costs line means more cost. September is column K. FCAST10 = FCAST09 + Fx + Operational Adjustments.

Business Month-End Story skill instructions
Business Month-End Story

Purpose: Take my business's Month and YTD comparative P&L (PL tab, amounts in thousands) from raw report to finished commentary, step by step, writing everything into the Story sheet.

Inputs I provide: the month; Capital Employed (actual, vs BUD, vs PY); last month's commentary as a style example; any answers from my forecast file.

Rules:
- The PL tab mixes GL account rows (labels start with a 4-digit account) and subtotal rows. Use subtotal rows for totals and account rows for drivers. Never add both.
- Month columns: F Actual, G Budget, H Var $, K PY, L Var vs PY $. YTD: O Actual, P Budget, Q Var $, T PY, U Var vs PY $.
- Sales and profit: positive variance = favorable. Costs: positive variance = unfavorable.
- A large month variance with a near-zero YTD variance is a timing item or reversal, not a saving. Call it out.
- Style: Sales as % of BUD and % of PY; GM% and OpEx% in bps ("c.446bps<BUD"); drivers in $m; max 60 words per paragraph.
- Only use reasons that are in the workbook or that I give you. Otherwise write "reason to confirm".

Steps:
1. Five most important findings -> Story rows 6-10.
2. Eight largest account drivers with Fav/Unfav, type and one question each -> Story rows 14-21.
3. PBIT bridge from budget to actual (volume, margin rate, Selling, D&W, G&A) on a PBIT_Bridge sheet.
4. Month and YTD commentary (Sales, GM%, OpEx%, Capital Employed) and three exec bullets -> Story rows 37-47.
5. Numbers to verify: every figure and the cell it came from.

Glossary

FCAST09 / FCAST10Forecast with actuals through August / September plus forecast for the rest of the year.
Operational AdjustmentsThe changes on the Adjustments tab that move FCAST09 to FCAST10 (excluding FX).
PBIT bridgeBudget profit to actual profit, split into volume, margin rate and cost effects.
bpsBasis points: 100bps = 1 percentage point.
Capital EmployedWorking capital plus net fixed assets.
Inventory / AR / AP daysBalance divided by monthly cost of sales or sales, times 30.
R&OsRisks and opportunities not in the forecast, weighted by likelihood (Low 25%, Medium 50%, High 75%).
Timing itemA variance that reverses in another month, so it nets to roughly zero year to date.
