Reference

Welcome

This guide is yours to keep. The practice files are look-alikes of the reports you already work with: the Month and YTD comparative P&L, the business forecast file with its Adjustments tab, the consolidated forecast, the 3-year financials and the Monthly Exec Summary. The layout is familiar; the company and every number are invented. Every prompt has a Copy button.

We are not here to turn accountants into AI experts. We are here to remove work you should not have to do manually.

What you will do

September is closed at Zava Safety, a fictitious division with eight businesses. The financial tables came out of the system automatically. The story did not. Using Copilot, you will:

  1. Get a quick win: ask the comparative P&L what matters most.
  2. Work smarter in Excel: make a system report Copilot-friendly, find the biggest movements, trace them to a business and get Copilot to explain formulas.
  3. Tell the forecast story: roll up every business's FCAST10 adjustments, check that the consolidation ties, and write the Safety forecast narrative.
  4. Find the actual vs budget drivers: write the September Monthly Commentary in the house style (% of BUD, bps, $m).
  5. Make it repeatable: turn it into a Month & YTD Variance Story skill and test it on August, where you already have the finished exec summary to compare against.
  6. Plan your month-end: pick one real task to try with Copilot next cycle.

Learning objectives

  • Choose between Chat, Plan and Edit in Copilot in Excel.
  • Get reliable answers from system-generated reports (subtotal rows, merged headers, metadata rows).
  • Find material variances, unusual postings and items that net to zero.
  • Draft Month and YTD commentary and forecast narratives in the house style.
  • Validate AI output: tie-outs, source cells, and "the data doesn't say".
  • Turn a monthly analysis into a reusable skill.

2. Put the files where Copilot can work with them

  1. Upload the files to your OneDrive (for example a folder called Copilot Workshop).
  2. Open them in Excel for the web (type excel.new in your browser, or open from OneDrive). Check that AutoSave is on.
  3. Select Copilot on the Home ribbon to open the Copilot pane.
Why Excel for the web today?
New Copilot features reach Excel for the web first. Desktop Excel updates on your company's schedule, so options like Plugins and skills may show up there a little later.

3. Get to know the Copilot pane

Chat

Questions, explanations and drafts. Your workbook is not changed.

Plan

Copilot lists the steps first; you approve them. Best for multi-step work you want to check.

Edit

Shown as Allow editing. Copilot changes the workbook: new sheets, formulas, tables, charts. Ctrl+Z undoes.

4. Ground rules

You own the output
Copilot gets you to a strong first draft faster. You remain accountable for every number and every word. You can outsource the processing, but not the responsibility.
Using your real files
The same prompts work on your own reports. Keep them in your Bunzl OneDrive or SharePoint, open them with your work account, and follow Bunzl's data policies. Don't paste real results into personal or public tools.

Zava Safety is a fictitious safety and PPE division with eight businesses and a regional office. Each business sends a comparative P&L and a forecast file every month; you consolidate them for leadership.

House rules hidden in the files

  • Two kinds of rows in the comparative P&L: GL account rows (for example 5300-2530 - TEMPORARY LABOR) and subtotal rows (for example Payroll Exp - D&W, Total Sales). Add them both up and you double count.
  • Forecast files show Op Costs as negative numbers, and variances as Favorable / (Unfavorable). On the Adjustments tab, a positive Op Costs adjustment means more cost.
  • FCAST09 = actuals through August + forecast. FCAST10 = actuals through September + forecast. FCAST10 = FCAST09 + Fx changes + Operational Adjustments.
  • The 3-year file is in whole dollars; everything else is in $000s.
  • Forecast data tabs start with grey system rows (Scenario#, Period# ...). They are part of the report retrieve, not data.
Things worth finding
The files contain a few things a good controller would catch: an early dropship order, a reallocation that nets to zero, a one-time credit, adjustments with no description, a business that submitted nothing, and a consolidation that doesn't quite tie. Let Copilot help you find them.

Follow-up promptChat mode
For each point, show me the row label and the exact cells you used, so I can check them.
What to expect

Wording varies, but you should see points like these:

  • Total Sales $53.6m, +$3.0m (+5.9%) vs budget and +$5.1m vs prior year.
  • Gross Margin % 29.7% vs 31.4% budget (about 170bps lower), so Gross Margin $ is only flat to budget despite the extra sales.
  • Operating Costs are slightly below budget (-$59K) even with higher sales; Operating Profit / PBIT $4.98m, +$84K vs budget.

Did Copilot double count?
If a number looks about twice as big as it should, Copilot probably added account rows and subtotal rows together. Tell it: "Use only the subtotal rows such as Total Sales and Gross Margin." You'll fix this for good in Lab 2.1.

Try this promptChat mode

Forecast Changes: Tell the Story

30 minutesFCAST09 to FCAST10
The story

Every business has submitted its FCAST10 file. Each adjustment line is supposed to carry the why. You need two things for leadership: the story by business and the overall Safety story for Sales, Margin and Opex. First one business, then the whole division.

3.1Tell one business's forecast story
Time 6 minGoal Summarize an Adjustments tabUse Granite forecast file > AdjustmentsLevel Intermediate
Try this promptChat mode
On the Adjustments tab, summarize every Section 1 adjustment with a non-zero total: Sales lines (rows 6-13), Gross Margin $ lines (rows 28-35) and Op Costs $ lines (rows 50-57). A positive Op Costs adjustment means more cost. Show a table with the description, months affected and full-year amount, then the total change in Sales, Gross Margin and Op Costs vs FCAST09, and the PBITA impact (Gross Margin minus Op Costs).
What to expect

Granite totals vs FCAST09: Sales -$1,180K (backorders -$700K and -$300K, rebate true-up -$180K), Gross Margin -$525K, Op Costs -$174K (savings from headcount, OT and backorder variable costs, partly offset by temp labor at the new DC), PBITA -$351K.

Self-check
3.2Spot the trap in the PBITA rows
Time 4 minGoal Don't trust a label you haven't checkedUse Granite > Adjustments rows 60-69Level Intermediate
Try this promptChat mode
Look at the PBITA section on the Adjustments tab (rows 61-68). For each row, show its formula and whether the Gross Margin and Op Costs lines it uses have the same description as the PBITA row label. Flag any mismatch.
What to expect

The PBITA rows are calculated by position (row 61 = row 28 - row 50, row 62 = row 29 - row 51...) and take their labels from the Sales rows. So "Rebate true-up - buying group" shows -$228K: the -$180K rebate plus $48K of temp labor. Row 63 "Backorders" mixes container freight and OT. Rows 64-65 have values but are labelled "Insert Adj."

Real-life lesson
If you ask any AI (or a new analyst) for "the PBITA impact of the rebate true-up" from these rows, you'll get the wrong answer with total confidence. Build PBITA by pairing Gross Margin and Op Costs lines with the same description, and put that rule in your skill.

The story

What to expect

Month: Sales 106% of BUD. Up: Hollis (120% of BUD, dropship timing), Falcon (114%), Beacon (112%), Alder (105%). Down: Ember (86%, backorders), Granite (88%). GM% -172bps: Cobalt (-490bps), Granite (-446bps), Alder (-366bps), Hollis (-251bps, dropship mix). OpEx% -133bps vs BUD.

YTD: Sales 102% of BUD; Falcon (+$5.9m) and Beacon (+$3.2m) lead; Granite (-$2.8m) and Ember (-$1.5m) lag. GM% -28bps; OpEx% -37bps.

4.2Separate one-time, reclass, timing and run-rate
Time 6 minGoal Catch issues before sign-offUse Comparative P&L SEP > PL_Flat and business tabsLevel Intermediate
Try this promptChat mode
For September, list operating cost items where an account is more than $100K away from budget at business level. For each, say whether it looks like a one-time item, a reclass that nets to zero within the business, timing, or a run-rate change, and give one follow-up question for the business controller. If the workbook doesn't show the reason, say so.
What to expect
  • Falcon Occupancy Allocation: +$420K G&A / -$420K D&W. Reclass, nets to zero. Ask: is this the right split going forward?
  • Alder Insurance: -$380K credit. One-time (recovery or accrual release?). Exclude it from the run-rate.
  • Hollis Temporary Labor (+$150K) and Falcon Temporary Labor (+$110K). Peak season: how long will it last?
  • Alder Selling Bonuses (+$140K): accrual true-up?
  • Hollis salaries (warehouse, sales and admin) about -$220K together: restructuring savings, a run-rate change. Each line on its own is under $100K, so ask Copilot to group by business too.
  • For the "why", a good answer says the P&L doesn't explain it.
Why this matters for OpEx%
OpEx% is 133bps better than budget, but about $380K of that is a one-time credit. Good commentary says so.

Month & YTD Variance Story skill instructions
Month & YTD Variance Story

Purpose: Turn a Month and YTD comparative P&L (consolidated PL tab plus one PL-<business> tab per business; amounts in thousands) into the Monthly Exec Summary commentary.

Inputs: the month (for example "August 2026"). Capital Employed (actual, vs BUD, vs PY) is provided by me because it is not in the P&L.

Rules:
- The PL tabs mix GL account rows (labels starting with a 4-digit account) and subtotal rows. Use subtotal rows (Total Sales, Gross Margin, Gross Margin %, Selling Costs, Delivery and Warehouse Costs, General & Admin Costs, Operating Costs, Operating Costs %, Operating Profit) for totals and account rows for drivers. Never add both.
- Sales, Gross Margin and Operating Profit: positive variance = favorable. Costs: positive variance = unfavorable.
- Express Sales as % of BUD and % of PY; GM% and OpEx% differences in bps; drivers in $m with one decimal.
- Material driver = at least $100K and 5% at business level.
- Treat items that offset within a business (for example Occupancy G&A vs D&W) as reclasses that net to zero. Call out one-time items separately from run-rate items.
- Only state reasons that are supported by the workbook or by context I give you. Otherwise write "reason to confirm with <business>".

Steps:
1. Consolidated Month and YTD: Sales, GM%, OpEx%, Operating Profit vs BUD and PY.
2. By business: the top favorable and unfavorable contributors to Sales, GM% and OpEx%.
3. Account drivers inside those businesses, plus one-time items, reclasses and timing.
4. Draft the Monthly Commentary: four paragraphs (Sales, GM%, OpEx%, Capital Employed), max 60 words each, in the house style ("102% of BUD", "c.47bps<BUD").
5. Draft the YTD Commentary in the same format.
6. Follow-ups: questions for business controllers.
7. Numbers to verify: every figure used and the cell it came from.

Save it as a skill: in the Copilot pane select +, then Plugins and skills, create a custom skill named Month & YTD Variance Story and paste the instructions.

Forecast Adjustment Roll-up skill instructions
Forecast Adjustment Roll-up

Purpose: Summarize all forecast adjustments in a consolidated forecast workbook (one -Adj tab per business plus a Safety-Adj summary tab) and tell the forecast-change story.

Rules:
- Months are columns C (January) to N (December); column O is the full-year total. Only include lines with a non-zero total.
- Section 1 rows: Sales items 6-13 (total row 14), Gross Margin $ items 28-35 (total 36), Op Costs $ items 50-57 (total 58). A positive Op Costs adjustment means MORE cost. PBITA impact = Gross Margin $ - Op Costs $.
- Do not use the PBITA section (rows 61-68) for descriptions: those rows are calculated by position and may not match the description.
- Flag any line described as "Insert Adj. n" (no description) and any business with no adjustments at all.

Steps:
1. Build a table: Business, Section (Sales / Gross Margin / Op Costs), Description, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec, Full year.
2. Summarize by business: Sales, Gross Margin $, Op Costs $, PBITA impact.
3. Tie-out: compare each business total with the matching row on Safety-Adj, and the Safety-Adj totals with the sum of businesses. Report every difference and the cell that causes it.
4. Story: one paragraph for the division (lead with the PBITA change vs the prior forecast) and one or two sentences per business, using the descriptions.
5. Open questions: missing descriptions, missing submissions, tie-out breaks, with a suggested owner.

Output: the tables, the story, then the open questions. Never invent a reason that is not in a description.

Troubleshooting

Copilot's totals are about double what I expect

It added account rows and subtotal rows together. Tell it which rows to use, or build the flat table from Lab 2.1 first.

Copilot can't see the R&Os sheet

It's hidden in the consolidated file (as in many real files). Right-click a sheet tab > Unhide, or ask Copilot in Edit mode to unhide it.

Copilot can't read my PowerPoint or another workbook

Copilot in Excel works on the workbook you have open. Paste the text you need into the prompt, as in Lab 4.3, or use Copilot in PowerPoint for the deck.

Big workbook, slow or vague answers

Name the sheet, rows and columns. "On Safety-Adj, row 39, column O" beats "in the forecast".

I don't see Plugins and skills in desktop Excel

Use Excel for the web, or keep your skill instructions in a document and paste them in.

My answer is different from my neighbor's

Normal. Compare the substance, check the numbers, keep the better prompt.

Glossary

FCAST09 / FCAST10Forecast with actuals through August / September plus forecast for the rest of the year.
Operational AdjustmentsThe changes each business enters on its Adjustments tab to move FCAST09 to FCAST10 (excluding FX).
Through Sep / Rest of YearActual months closed so far / remaining forecast months.
PBITAGross Margin minus Op Costs. Op Costs are negative in the forecast files.
bpsBasis points: 100bps = 1 percentage point.
Capital EmployedWorking capital (inventory, receivables, less payables) plus net fixed assets.
R&OsRisks and opportunities not in the forecast, weighted by likelihood (Low 25%, Medium 50%, High 75%).
Subtotal rowsRows like "Payroll Exp - D&W" or "Total Sales" that add up the account rows above them.
