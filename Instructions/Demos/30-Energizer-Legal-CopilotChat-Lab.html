Overview

In our September 28 planning call, the Legal and Trade Compliance teams described the same pressures again and again: business requesters who don't know which agreement they need, third-party paper with urgent deadlines, prior advice buried in email, repetitive trade questions, and manual monitoring of regulatory change. This lab shows how far you can get on each of those today with Microsoft Copilot Chat, which is included with your Microsoft 365 license.

You will

  • Review third-party paper against a playbook
  • Turn vague requests into a clear intake
  • Research regulations with citations
  • Ask your inbox what you said before
  • Analyze a contract register and draft deliverables

Learning objectives

  • Know what Copilot Chat can and can't see
  • Add files three ways: upload, / reference, or Outlook
  • Pick the right response mode
  • Write prompts with goal, context, source, and expectations
  • Verify output before relying on it

You will produce

  • A one-page issues list for a vendor MSA
  • An intake checklist for business requesters
  • A regulatory impact note with sources
  • A triaged list of open legal requests
  • A renewal watchlist and leadership summary
Copilot assists your judgment. It doesn't replace it.Nothing Copilot produces is legal advice. Attorneys and compliance professionals remain responsible for every conclusion, every redline, and every communication. Treat Copilot output like a first draft from a capable new team member: useful, fast, and in need of review.

Ground rules for legal content
  • Follow Energizer's AI and information-handling policies. Use the sample files for practice. Use real matters only where your policy allows.
  • Copilot Chat prompts and responses are logged and retained under your organization's Microsoft Purview policies and are available for audit and eDiscovery. Draft prompts with the same care you would use in email, particularly for privileged matters.
  • Copilot respects your existing permissions. It can't show you files or emails you can't already open.
  • Always open the citations. Verify every date, threshold, citation, and quotation before you rely on it.

Prompting for legal work

Strong prompts have four parts. You'll see this pattern in every lab.

1. Goal

What you need. "Identify the five highest-risk clauses…"

2. Context

Who it's for and why. "…for a supply-chain director who must sign by Friday."

3. Source

What Copilot should use. "Use only the attached MSA and our playbook."

4. Expectations

Format, tone, and limits. "Return a table with a section reference for each finding. If a term isn't in the document, write 'not found.'"
Three habits that improve accuracyAsk for section references or quotes. Tell Copilot to say "not found" instead of guessing. Ask a follow-up question rather than starting over.

Scenario A · Vendor paper on a deadline

Deliverable: an issues list with fallback language and an email to the business owner

The story. At 4:40 p.m. on Thursday, Supply Chain forwards a 3PL Master Services Agreement from Brightline Logistics. It's on Brightline's paper, the go-live date is November 1, and the director wants to sign tomorrow. You have the department playbook and an hour. You need to know what's in the agreement, where it departs from your positions, and what to tell the business.
StakeholderSupply Chain director
Time pressureSignature requested within 24 hours
SourcesVendor MSA + playbook
DeliverableIssues list + business email
A1Get the deal on one page▸
⏱ 5 minUpload a fileFile: Brightline_Logistics_MSA_SAMPLE.docxBeginner

Before negotiating, you need the shape of the deal: parties, term, money, exit rights, and risk allocation. Copilot reads the full agreement in seconds and gives you a structured summary you can check against the source.

  1. In Copilot Chat, select + → Upload, or drag the MSA into the chat box.
  2. Paste the prompt below.
Try this prompt
You are supporting an in-house commercial attorney. Using only the attached Master Services Agreement, create a one-page deal summary for a supply-chain director.

Return a table with these rows: Parties, Services, Effective date, Initial term, Renewal mechanics and notice deadline, Pricing and price-increase rights, Payment terms, Service levels and remedies, Data rights, Liability cap, Indemnity, Insurance, Termination rights, Governing law and disputes, Assignment and subcontracting.

For each row, include a plain-English summary and the section number. If something isn't addressed in the agreement, write "Not addressed." Don't add information that isn't in the document.
What to expectA table of about 15 rows with section references, such as 2.2: auto-renews for 3-year terms unless notice is given 120 days before the term ends, and 8.2: liability capped at 3 months of fees.
VerifyOpen the document and spot-check at least three section references, especially dates and dollar amounts.

Follow-up:

Follow-up
What important protections would a customer normally expect in a logistics services agreement that are missing from this document? List them with a one-sentence explanation of why each matters.
  • I can find each summarized term in the source document.
  • Copilot said "Not addressed" for missing items instead of inventing them.
A2Check it against the playbook▸
⏱ 7 minReference a file with /File: Commercial_Contracting_Playbook_SAMPLE.docxIntermediate

A summary tells you what the agreement says. A playbook comparison tells you what to do about it. This is the manual first pass that often eats an afternoon.

  1. In the same chat, type / and start typing Commercial_Contracting. Select the playbook from your OneDrive. If it doesn't appear, upload it with +.
  2. Paste the prompt.
Try this prompt
Compare the Brightline Master Services Agreement against our Commercial Contracting Playbook.

For every playbook topic, return a table with these columns:
- Topic
- What the MSA says (with section number)
- Playbook standard position
- Rating: Green (meets standard), Yellow (within fallback), or Red (escalation trigger)
- Why it matters to the business, in one sentence

Sort Red items first. Then list the top five issues I should raise first in negotiation and explain why. Base every rating only on the two documents.
What to expectMost topics should be Red, including 120-day renewal notice, unilateral 9% increases, a 3-month liability cap, a perpetual data license, $0.50/lb goods liability, Nevada arbitration, missing trade-compliance clauses, and incorporated online terms.
Trade compliance angleAsk: "Which export control, sanctions, anti-bribery, and forced-labor provisions does the playbook require that this MSA lacks? Note where subcontracting outside the U.S. increases that risk."
  • Every Red item maps to an escalation trigger in the playbook.
  • I reviewed the ratings myself and changed any I disagree with.
A3Think deeper on the liability cap▸
⏱ 5 minResponse mode: Think deeperSame chatIntermediate

Some questions need reasoning, not lookup. Liability allocation in a logistics deal interacts with goods value, insurance, indemnity, and data exposure. Switch the response mode so Copilot takes more time to plan and check its work.

  1. Open the model selector at the top of the chat and choose Think deeper.
  2. Paste the prompt.
Try this prompt
Think through how sections 7, 8, 9, and 11 of the Brightline MSA work together.

Assume we store an average of $4 million in battery inventory at Brightline's warehouse and annual fees are about $1.25 million. Walk through three scenarios:
1. A warehouse fire destroys $2 million of inventory.
2. A security incident at Brightline exposes our retailer shipment data.
3. Brightline misses on-time shipment targets for two straight quarters during the holiday season.

For each scenario, estimate what we could realistically recover under the current language and what we could recover under the playbook's standard position. Show your assumptions and calculations. End with the two changes that would reduce our exposure most.
VerifyScenario math depends on assumptions. Check the arithmetic and treat the output as an issue-spotting aid, not a damages analysis.
Talking pointThis is a good moment to compare Quick response and Think deeper. Run the same prompt both ways if time allows.
A4Fallback language + business email▸
⏱ 6 minDraftingSame chatIntermediate

Close the loop: propose language the vendor can accept, and tell the business owner, in plain words, why signing tomorrow isn't advisable and what happens next.

Try this prompt
For the five Red issues you prioritized, draft proposed replacement language for each clause that reflects the playbook's standard position. Also draft a fallback that reflects the Yellow position. Present each as:
- Section
- Current language (short quote)
- Proposed language
- Fallback language
- One-line rationale we can share with the vendor

Keep the drafting concise and consistent with a U.S. commercial agreement.
Then draft the business email
Now write a short email from me to the Supply Chain director. Explain in plain language, with no legal jargon:

- What we'll ask Brightline to change

Keep it under 200 words, with a confident and helpful tone.

Try this prompt
Act as a legal intake coordinator. Use the "Agreement type selector" and "Minimum intake information" sections of the attached playbook.

Here is the request we received:
"Hi Legal - we need an MSA with Northwind Creative ASAP so they can start our holiday social campaign on Nov 1. They'll manage about 12 influencers on TikTok and Instagram, and we want to reuse the best videos in paid ads through Q1. Budget is about $85K. They sent their contract, but it's long, so can we just use ours? Thanks! - Priya, Brand Marketing"

What to expectCopilot should recommend a Marketing / Agency Services Agreement, flag missing items such as legal entity, country, and personal data, and raise usage rights for repurposing influencer content in paid media.

Process:
