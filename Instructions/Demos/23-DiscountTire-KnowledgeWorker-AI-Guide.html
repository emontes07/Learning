Your AI workday

Same tools you know. A completely different way to use them.

You already live in Microsoft 365. You check email in Outlook, join calls in Teams, crunch numbers in Excel, write in Word, and present in PowerPoint. Copilot doesn't add a new app to your day — it makes every one of those apps dramatically faster.

Think about your last week. How much time did you spend summarizing email threads? Formatting spreadsheets? Staring at a blank page trying to start a document? Building slides from scratch? Scrolling through a chat thread trying to find what was decided? That's the time Copilot gives back to you — not by doing your job, but by handling the parts of your job that slow you down.

"Copilot doesn't replace what you do. It removes the friction between what you know and getting it done."

How to use this guide

Each section below covers one M365 app. Every section has real prompts you can copy and paste right now. Start with the app you use most — you don't need to go in order. Try one prompt today, another tomorrow. Build the habit gradually.

1
Outlook · Email, reinvented

Stop reading every email. Start reading only what matters.

The average knowledge worker spends 28% of their workday on email. Copilot in Outlook cuts through the noise so you focus on what needs action.

Before Copilot

Open a 14-message thread. Read every reply. Figure out what was decided. Compose a careful response. 15 minutes.

With Copilot

Click "Summarize." Read the 3-sentence summary. Hit "Draft reply." Edit two sentences. Send. 2 minutes.

Summarize

Long threads → key points

Open any email thread and click Summary by Copilot at the top. Copilot reads every message and gives you the highlights: what was discussed, what was decided, and what's still open.

Works on any thread with 3+ messages.
Draft

Replies in your voice

Click the Copilot icon while composing and describe what you want to say. Copilot writes the draft. You adjust the tone — professional, casual, direct, diplomatic — and hit send.

Adjust length and tone with one click.
Coach

Better writing, instantly

Wrote your own reply? Ask Copilot to review it: "Is this clear?" "Is the tone right for a VP audience?" "Can you make this more concise?" Coaching before you hit send.

Like a proofreader who's always available.
Try these prompts
Summarize a thread
Summarize this email thread. What was the original ask? What's been decided so far? What's still pending?
Draft a reply
Draft a reply confirming I can attend the meeting on Thursday. Ask if they can share the agenda ahead of time so I can prepare. Keep it professional but friendly.
Draft a new email
Write an email to my team summarizing this week's priorities. Include: the Q3 report deadline on Friday, the all-hands on Wednesday, and a reminder to complete their training by end of month. Keep it upbeat and under 150 words.
Adjust tone
Make this more concise and direct. The reader is a senior leader who wants the bottom line first, then the details.
Time saved

A 30-minute inbox session becomes 10 minutes. Replies are better written, faster, and you never miss the key decision buried in message 11 of a 14-message thread.

Presenter note

Open a real long email thread and hit "Summarize" — the instant summary is the quickest win. Then show "Draft with Copilot" for a reply. These two features are what people use on Day 1 and never stop using.

2
Teams · Meetings & chats

Meetings that drive action — not meetings that drain your calendar.

The meeting itself isn't the problem. The problem is everything before and after: the prep you didn't have time for, the notes nobody took, the follow-up that never happened.

During the meeting

Copilot joins the meeting and listens — privately. You can ask it questions during the meeting without anyone else seeing your prompts. Late to a call? "What's been discussed so far?" Zoned out for a minute? "What did Sarah just suggest?"

Live catch-up

Jump in without being lost

"Catch me up — what's been discussed in the last 10 minutes?" Copilot gives you a fast summary so you can contribute immediately, not sit silently for 5 minutes trying to figure out what you missed.

Live questions

Ask without interrupting

"What questions are still unresolved?" or "Has anyone mentioned the budget?" — ask Copilot instead of derailing the conversation. Your prompts are private to you.

During-meeting prompts
Catch up mid-meeting
What has been discussed so far? Summarize the key points and any decisions that have been made.
Track action items in real time
What action items have been mentioned so far? List each one with the person responsible.
Check for unresolved topics
Are there any open questions or disagreements that haven't been resolved yet?

After the meeting

This is where the real magic is. Instead of a raw transcript nobody reads, Copilot gives you a structured recap in any format you want — decisions, action items, key topics. You can even ask it to draft the follow-up email.

Recap

Custom-format summaries

Ask for the recap your way: "List only the decisions and owners" or "Give me a recap I can paste into our project tracker." Same transcript, different outputs for different needs.

Follow-up

Draft the post-meeting email

"Draft a follow-up email with the action items and next steps." Copilot writes it from the meeting content. You review and send. The follow-up goes out in 2 minutes, not 2 days.

Missed it?

Catch up without the recording

Couldn't attend? Open the meeting in Teams and ask Copilot what happened. A 60-minute meeting becomes a 60-second summary. No playback. No scrolling through a transcript.

After-meeting prompts
Custom recap
Give me a recap organized as: (1) Decisions made — specific commitments, not themes; (2) Action items — who owns it, what they need to do, and the deadline; (3) Key discussion points; (4) Open questions to follow up on.
Draft a follow-up email
Draft a follow-up email to the attendees. Include the key decisions, action items with owners, and next steps. Keep it professional and under 200 words.
Catch up on a missed meeting
I missed this meeting. Give me a summary of the main topics discussed, any decisions that were made, and any action items that involve me or my team.

In chats and channels

Copilot works in Teams chats too — not just meetings. Catch up on any conversation in seconds.

Catch up on a chat
Summarize this conversation. What were the key points? Are there any decisions or action items I need to know about?
Time saved

The 20-minute post-meeting ritual — re-read notes, type up action items, send follow-up — becomes 3 minutes. And the follow-up is better written and more complete than anything you'd have typed from memory.

Presenter note

The custom recap is the headliner — show it from a real recent meeting if possible. The format flexibility ("give me just the decisions and owners") is what hooks people. If you only show one thing in Teams, show the after-meeting recap.

Excel · Data & analysis

Ask questions about your data in plain English. Get answers in seconds.

You don't need to be an Excel expert to get expert-level analysis. Describe what you want to know — Copilot does the formulas, the formatting, and the charts.

Before Copilot

Google the VLOOKUP syntax. Try it. Get an error. Google the error. Try again. 20 minutes later, the formula works. Build a pivot table. Format the chart. 45 minutes total.

With Copilot

"Show me the top 10 by revenue with a bar chart." Done. "Add a column flagging anything below target." Done. "What trends should I know about?" Answer in 10 seconds.

Ask

Questions in plain language

"What's the average by region?" "Which rows are missing data?" "What's the trend over the last 6 months?" — ask questions like you'd ask a coworker. Copilot reads your data and answers.

No formula syntax required.
Create

Formulas and new columns

"Add a column that calculates year-over-year growth" — Copilot writes the formula and applies it. Complex logic like IF/AND/VLOOKUP, described in words instead of code.

Always review the formula before using it.
Visualize

Charts and highlights

"Create a chart showing monthly trends" — Copilot picks the right chart type, formats it, and inserts it. Need conditional formatting? "Highlight anything below 80% in red."

Ask for specific chart types if you prefer.
Try these prompts
Analyze your data
What are the top 10 rows by total revenue? Show me a summary with any notable trends or outliers.
Add a calculated column
Add a new column called 'Status'. Mark each row as 'On Track' if the actual value is at least 90% of the target, 'At Risk' if between 75-90%, and 'Behind' if below 75%.
Create a chart
Create a bar chart comparing actual vs. target for each category. Use green for bars that met or exceeded target and red for those below target.
Spot insights
Analyze this data and tell me the 3 most important insights. What patterns do you see? What should I pay attention to? Are there any anomalies?
Sort and filter
Sort this table by the 'Revenue' column in descending order. Then filter to show only rows where the region is 'West' or 'Southwest'.
Always verify

Copilot writes formulas and performs calculations — but you own the numbers. Spot-check a few results before sharing with your team or leadership. The AI handles the heavy lifting; your judgment makes it trustworthy.

Time saved

The 45-minute "clean, format, pivot, chart" ritual becomes 5 minutes. And the person who used to spend their morning wrestling with formulas now spends it interpreting what the data means — which is the real job.

Presenter note

If you have a sample spreadsheet, the "top 10 with a chart" prompt is fast and visual. The "add a calculated column" prompt shows formula generation — always a crowd-pleaser for people who struggle with Excel formulas. Remind the room: Copilot works on data in tables — press Ctrl+T to format data as a table first.

Word · Write & edit

The blank page is the enemy. Copilot eliminates it.

Whether it's a report, a proposal, an SOP, or a status update — the hardest part is always the first draft. Copilot handles the 80% that's structure and phrasing so you can focus on the 20% that's your expertise.

Before Copilot

Stare at blank page. Write an outline. Start drafting. Get stuck. Delete a paragraph. Start over. 90 minutes for a first draft that's… okay.

With Copilot

Describe what you need. Copilot drafts the structure and prose. You refine, add your expertise, adjust the tone. 20 minutes for a draft that's better than "okay."

Draft

First drafts in minutes

Describe what you need — a report, a proposal, a summary, a communication — and Copilot writes the first draft with structure, headers, and professional prose. You make it yours.

Works with a blank doc or an existing one.
Rewrite

Select text, transform it

Select any paragraph and ask Copilot to make it shorter, change the tone, simplify the language, or expand a bullet into a full section. Instant editing without the rewriting pain.

Adjust tone: formal, casual, confident, empathetic.
Reference

Write from existing files

"Draft a summary based on /Q3 Performance Report.docx" — Copilot reads your existing files and uses them as source material. Same data, new document, fraction of the time.

Reference files in OneDrive or SharePoint with /.
Try these prompts
Draft from scratch
Write a project status update for my manager. Include sections for: What we accomplished this week, What's in progress, Blockers or risks, and Next week's priorities. Keep it clear and concise — no more than one page.
Draft from a reference file
Based on /[your file name], write a one-page executive summary. Lead with the key findings, then the recommended actions, then the supporting data. Keep it at an executive level — no jargon, no deep technical detail.
Rewrite for a different audience
Rewrite this section for a non-technical audience. Remove the jargon, use plain language, and keep the key message. It should be understandable by someone outside our department.
Make it shorter
Make this document more concise. Cut the length by about half while keeping all the key points and recommendations. Tighten the language — every sentence should earn its place.
Write a process document
Write a standard operating procedure for [describe your process]. Include: Purpose, Scope, Step-by-step instructions with numbered steps, Roles and responsibilities, and Exceptions or special cases. Use clear, direct language that a new team member could follow on their first day.
Power tip — the / reference

When drafting in Word, type / in the Copilot prompt to reference another file from your OneDrive or SharePoint. This lets you say "draft a summary based on this existing report" — and Copilot reads the source and writes from it. Huge time saver for creating executive summaries, proposals from RFPs, or reports from raw data docs.

Time saved

A first draft that used to take 60–90 minutes takes 10. The document that sat on your to-do list for three days because you couldn't face the blank page? Done before lunch. And the quality of the first draft means less revision later.

Presenter note

The "project status update" prompt is universally relatable — everyone writes these. Open a blank Word doc and demo it live. The /reference feature is the advanced move that impresses — if you have a OneDrive file to reference, show it. Otherwise, narrate the concept and move on.

PowerPoint · Present & persuade

From idea to polished deck — without the death-by-slide-building.

Nobody's job is "build slides." But somehow, building slides takes hours every week. Copilot creates the deck so you can focus on the story you want to tell.

Before Copilot

Find last quarter's deck. Copy and rename it. Delete old content. Rearrange slides. Write new content. Fix formatting. Adjust the layout. Add speaker notes. 2-3 hours.

With Copilot

"Create a presentation about…" First draft in 60 seconds. Add slides, adjust content, write speaker notes — all from prompts. Polish and present. 30 minutes.

Create

Decks from a description

Describe the presentation you need and Copilot creates it — title slide, agenda, content slides, and closing — with a coherent structure and professional design.

Applies your org's template automatically.
From a doc

Turn a document into a deck

"Create a presentation from this Word document" — Copilot reads the doc, identifies the narrative, and structures it into slides. Your report becomes your presentation.

The Word → PPT flow is a game-changer.
Enhance

Add, edit, polish

"Add a slide about risks." "Write speaker notes for every slide." "Make this slide more visual." Copilot extends and refines the deck, slide by slide or all at once.

Speaker notes make you a better presenter.
Try these prompts
Create a deck from scratch
Create a presentation about our team's quarterly results. Include: Title slide, Agenda, Key Metrics (with placeholders for numbers), Top Wins this quarter, Challenges and what we're doing about them, Priorities for next quarter, and a Q&A slide. 8-10 slides, clean modern design.
Create from a Word document
Create a presentation based on /[your Word document]. Pull out the key points and organize them into a clear narrative. Keep it to 10 slides max. Each slide should make one point clearly — no walls of text.
Add a slide
Add a slide after this one that summarizes the 3 biggest risks we're tracking. Use a layout with icons or callout boxes. Keep text minimal — each risk should be one sentence with a brief mitigation note.
Write speaker notes
Write speaker notes for every slide in this deck. Each note should help the presenter tell a story — not just read the slide. Include key talking points, transition phrases between slides, and any data points worth emphasizing verbally.
Simplify a dense slide
This slide has too much text. Rewrite it to be more visual and concise. Keep the 3 most important points and remove everything else. Suggest a better layout if possible.
Time saved

A 10-slide deck that took 2 hours now takes 30 minutes. And the speaker notes mean you show up prepared — not reading bullets off the screen. The hours you used to spend building slides? That's now time for practice, for thinking about the story, for the work that makes the presentation actually land.

Presenter note

Two killer demos: (1) Open a blank PowerPoint and prompt "Create a presentation about…" — the 60-second deck generation always gets a reaction. (2) If you created a Word doc in the previous section, now say "Create a presentation from this document" in PowerPoint — the Word → PPT pipeline is the strongest cross-app moment. Pick one; both is even better if you have time.

Copilot Chat · Your AI assistant across M365

One place to ask anything — across all your files, emails, chats, and calendar.

Copilot Chat (formerly BizChat) sits above individual apps. It can search across your entire M365 world — emails, documents, meetings, chats — and give you answers grounded in your work data.

Think of Copilot Chat as your universal entry point. Instead of opening five apps to find five pieces of information, open Copilot Chat and ask one question. It searches across Outlook, Teams, OneDrive, SharePoint, and Calendar — and gives you an answer that references your actual work context.

Research

Find anything across M365

"What did our team decide about the Q3 timeline?" Copilot searches your emails, meetings, and chats to find the answer — even if you can't remember which app the conversation happened in.

Prepare

Meeting prep in 60 seconds

"Prepare me for my 2 PM meeting with the regional managers. What have we discussed recently? What files have been shared? What should I review?" All context, one prompt.

Catch up

After time off or a busy week

"What did I miss while I was out last Thursday and Friday? Highlight anything that needs my attention this week." Copilot reads across everything and gives you the briefing.

Try these prompts
Morning briefing
What should I focus on today? Summarize my most important emails from the last 24 hours, highlight anything that needs a decision from me, and list my meetings for today with any context I should know going in.
Meeting prep
Prepare me for my next meeting. What's on the agenda? What files or emails relate to this topic? What was discussed in our last meeting on this subject? What should I be ready to talk about?
Catch up after time off
I was out of office Monday and Tuesday. What did I miss? Summarize the most important emails, meetings, and chat messages. Highlight anything that needs my attention this week and any decisions that were made without me.
Find a past decision
When did we decide on the new process for [topic]? Search my emails, Teams chats, and meeting transcripts for the most recent discussion about this. Who was involved and what was decided?
Research a person before a meeting
I have a meeting with [person's name] tomorrow. What have they emailed me about recently? What files have they shared with me? What topics have we discussed in Teams? Give me a quick brief so I'm prepared.
Where to find Copilot Chat

Access Copilot Chat at m365.cloud.microsoft/chat, in the Microsoft 365 app, in the Teams sidebar, or from the Copilot icon in your Windows taskbar. Same experience, multiple entry points — use whichever is fastest for you.

Why this matters

You stop being the person who digs through five apps looking for one answer. You become the person who asks one question and gets a sourced, contextual answer in 10 seconds. The time you save isn't just minutes — it's the mental energy of searching that you redirect to thinking.

Presenter note

Copilot Chat is the "wow" moment for many people — the cross-app intelligence is what sets M365 Copilot apart from generic AI. Show the morning briefing prompt live (it uses real data from your tenant). The "catch up after time off" prompt is universally relatable. If someone says "I can do that with ChatGPT," this is the rebuttal: ChatGPT doesn't know your emails, meetings, and files.

When one person's productivity tip becomes the whole team's advantage.

Copilot makes you faster. Agents make your team's processes faster — automatically, consistently, and without someone having to remember every step.

There's a difference between "I'm more productive" and "our process is more productive." Everything above is about the first. Agents are about the second. An agent is an AI-powered assistant that's grounded on your content, connected to your systems, and available to your team — 24/7, consistent, and always citing sources.

"Think about the questions your team asks every week that always have the same answer. Now imagine those questions never reaching your inbox."

Set up in minutes. No code required.

Trust & practical tips

Everything you should know about safety, privacy, and getting the most out of Copilot.

🔒

Your access, your boundaries

Copilot can only see what you can already see. If you don't have access to a file or a SharePoint site, neither does Copilot. Existing permissions apply — nothing changes.

🛡

Your data stays yours

Your business data is not used to train Microsoft's AI models. Everything stays within your Microsoft 365 tenant. Period.

✋

You're always in charge

Copilot suggests. You decide. Every draft, every formula, every summary is a suggestion that you review before using. AI is the assistant; you own the final work.

💡

Better prompts = better results

Be specific. Give context. Tell Copilot the format, the audience, and the tone you want. "Write a professional summary of this meeting for my VP" beats "summarize this."

Quick-start tips for better prompts

The prompt formula

Goal + Context + Format + Tone = great results.

Example: "Write a status update [goal] about our Q3 hiring progress [context] as a bulleted email [format] that's professional but upbeat [tone]."

Iterate, don't start over

If the first result isn't right, refine it. "Make it shorter." "Add more detail about the timeline." "Change the tone to be more formal." Copilot remembers the conversation — each follow-up builds on the last.

The golden rule

Always review before you send, share, or present. Copilot gives you a strong first draft — but the final quality check is always yours. Verify numbers, check tone, confirm accuracy. The AI does the heavy lifting; your judgment makes it trustworthy.

Get started

Your first week with Copilot — four things to try.

You don't need to learn everything at once. Start with one habit, build from there. Here's a practical first-week plan.

Day 1

Summarize an email thread

Open the longest email thread in your inbox. Click "Summarize." See 14 messages become 3 sentences. You'll never read another long thread the old way.

Day 2

Recap a meeting

After your next Teams meeting, ask Copilot for a recap with decisions and action items. Compare it to the notes you would have taken. Notice the difference.

Day 3

Draft a document

The next time you need to write something — a status update, a process doc, an email to the team — start with Copilot. Describe what you need. Edit from there.

Day 4–5

Explore what matters to you

Try Copilot in the app you use most. Excel formulas? PowerPoint decks? Copilot Chat? Pick the workflow that costs you the most time and see how Copilot changes it.

The people who get the most from Copilot aren't the ones who learn every feature. They're the ones who find two or three things that save them time every day — and make them habits. Start small. Be consistent. The compound effect is real.
