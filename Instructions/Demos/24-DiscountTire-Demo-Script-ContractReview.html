Demo setup & context

The scenario: an urgent contract review lands in your lap.

You're a senior analyst at a drone and e-bike company. You cover multiple roles including suppliers and contract analysis. You provide reports to leadership — not just data, but interpretation and recommendations. This morning, you notice an urgent Teams chat: "Contract Review ASAP".

The demo follows your natural workflow: catch up on what you missed → bring in the right people → analyze the data in Excel → build the leadership report → confirm and prep for the decision meeting.

Before you start

Pre-requisites: Teams chat named "Contract Review ASAP" with existing messages · Excel file with contract data · Copilot added as an agent in the chat · Calendar event for the contract review meeting · Facilitator enabled in Teams.

"This isn't a feature tour. It's a real morning — the kind where AI turns an urgent request into a structured response, not a scramble."

Scene 1 · Teams · Catch up on what you missed

You see a notification — "Contract Review ASAP." What did you miss?

Instead of scrolling through 30 messages to figure out what happened, you ask Copilot to bring you up to speed in seconds.

You start your day and see the Teams chat "Contract Review ASAP" has been active. Multiple messages, urgency signals, and you need to get current — fast.

Step 1 — Open the chat

Navigate to "Contract Review ASAP" in Teams

Open the chat. Notice the messages you've missed. Instead of reading everything, open Copilot in the chat pane.

Step 2 — Ask Copilot to catch you up

Get a summary of the conversation

In the Copilot panel (or in the M365 Portal), ask for a summary of everything that's been discussed.

Try this in Teams — Copilot pane
Catch me up on this conversation. What's been discussed, what decisions have been made, and what's urgent?
Alternative — in Copilot Chat (Portal)
Summarize the Teams chat "Contract Review ASAP". What's the situation, who's involved, and what actions are pending?
What to expect

Copilot reads the full thread and gives you: what the contract issue is, who raised it, what's been discussed so far, and what's still pending. You're oriented in 30 seconds, not 10 minutes.

Key message for the audience

This works in the Teams chat pane and in the Copilot Chat portal — same intelligence, wherever you are. No scrolling, no re-reading, just instant context.

Presenter note

Show both options quickly — the Teams pane summary and mention the Portal. The fact that it works from both places reinforces the "Copilot is everywhere" message. Keep this to 2 minutes.

Add the right person and let Copilot handle the onboarding.

You notice Lisa Taylor isn't in the chat, but she should be — she needs to lead the contract evaluation and provide legal support. You add her and use the Copilot agent in the chat to bring her up to speed and set expectations.

Bring in the right person

Add Lisa Taylor to the "Contract Review ASAP" chat. Then use the Copilot agent (already added to the chat) to onboard her.

Let Copilot brief the new participant

Instead of typing out a summary yourself, ask Copilot to handle it — plus, inform the group about the upcoming meeting.

Presenter note

The @Copilot interaction in the chat is a big moment — it shows Copilot as a team member, not just a personal assistant. Emphasize that the response is visible to everyone. This differentiates from the private Copilot pane. 2-3 minutes on this scene.

Scene 3 · Excel · Model selection & Customize Copilot

Set up Copilot to work the way you work — before you even start analyzing.

Two powerful features most people don't know about: choosing the right AI model and teaching Copilot your role, your standards, and your formatting preferences.

Before diving into the contract data, you configure Copilot to work like your analyst — with your role context, your formatting standards, and your presentation expectations.

Feature spotlight

Model Selection

Show the audience that you can switch AI models depending on the task. Different models excel at different things — reasoning, speed, creativity. Pick the right tool for the job.

Step 1 — Show model selection

Change the model in Copilot

Open the model selector and show that you can choose between available models. Explain that different tasks benefit from different models.

Feature spotlight

Customize Copilot — Persistent Instructions

Tell Copilot who you are and how you work. These instructions persist across your session — every prompt benefits from this context without you repeating it.

Step 2 — Set custom instructions

Configure "Customize Copilot" with your role and formatting standards

Open the Customize Copilot settings and enter your professional context and formatting preferences.

Custom instructions to enter
I'm a senior analyst on a drone and e-bike company. I cover various roles including suppliers and contract analysis. I have to provide reports to leadership not just providing the data but also interpretation of the data. Show data with green/red fills and icons like "Check" or "X" whenever appropriate. Never let text get cut off: merge and wrap prose (reasons, notes); auto-fit data columns to their content once, and don't let a lower table resize a column a higher one already set.
What to expect

From this point forward, every Copilot response in Excel will follow these instructions — formatted for leadership, with conditional formatting, proper column widths, and interpretive analysis, not just raw data.

Key message for the audience

Customize Copilot means you set your context once and every interaction benefits. No more repeating "I'm an analyst" or "format it professionally" in every prompt. Copilot knows your role, your standards, and your audience — permanently.

Presenter note

Model selection is a quick show — 15 seconds. The Customize Copilot feature is the star here. Read the instructions aloud as you type them — the audience should hear the specificity. Then show that subsequent prompts automatically apply those standards. 3 minutes total.

Scene 4 · Excel · Skills & WorkIQ

Reusable skills, intelligent file discovery, and cross-app intelligence.

Skills teach Copilot how to do a specific recurring task consistently. WorkIQ finds the right files across your M365 environment without you hunting for them.

Feature spotlight

Skills — Reusable instructions for recurring work

Skills are reusable instructions or capabilities Copilot uses to complete recurring work consistently. Think of them as "saved expertise" — teach Copilot once how you do a specific analysis, and it applies that same methodology every time.

Step 1 — Show Skills

Demonstrate how to add a Skill

Show the audience the Skills feature. Explain that a Skill provides Copilot with specific instructions on how to perform a particular task — like a playbook it follows consistently.

What are Skills?

Skills = reusable instructions for Copilot. If you run the same type of analysis every month — contract risk scoring, vendor evaluation, budget variance — you create a Skill so Copilot does it the same way every time, with the same criteria, the same output format, and the same quality.

Feature spotlight

WorkIQ — Intelligent file discovery

When you reference content in a prompt, Copilot uses WorkIQ to find relevant files across your M365 environment. It suggests files based on your text — you don't need to know the exact file name or location.

Step 2 — Run the analysis prompt

Ask Copilot to find and analyze the contract data

Use a natural language prompt that references the data — Copilot uses WorkIQ to locate the file and suggests using it.

The power prompt — WorkIQ finds the file
Can you find the "contracts data" file and analyze all of them, is there any contract at risk? Create rich dashboards if possible. @buyer-list
What to expect

Copilot uses WorkIQ to locate the "contracts data" file from your M365 environment, suggests it for use, and then performs the analysis — identifying at-risk contracts and creating visual dashboards. The @buyer-list reference pulls in additional context from a Skill or data source.

Key message for the audience

You didn't need to find the file, open it, copy data, or build the dashboard manually. You described what you needed in plain English — and Copilot found the data, analyzed it, and created the output. This is the difference between using AI and working with AI.

Presenter note

The WorkIQ moment is magical — type the prompt and pause when Copilot suggests the file. Say "I didn't tell it where the file is. It found it based on the context." Then let the analysis run. The dashboard creation with green/red formatting (from your custom instructions) ties back to Scene 3. 3-4 minutes here.

Scene 5 · Excel · Leadership report & sensitivity labels

From analysis to leadership-ready report — with enterprise security built in.

Copilot helps you complete the report for leadership, and the sensitivity label is automatically applied — protecting the content from the moment it's created.

The analysis is done. Now you need the leadership report — not just the data, but the interpretation, the risks flagged, the recommendations clear. And because this involves contract data, the document must be properly classified.

Step 1 — Complete the report

Ask Copilot to help build the leadership report

With the analysis complete, ask Copilot to help you structure and write the report for leadership.

Build the leadership report
Help me complete the report for the leadership team. Summarize the key findings from the contract analysis, highlight the contracts at risk with specific reasons, and provide recommended actions. Include the visual dashboard. Format it for a VP-level audience — interpretation first, data second.
Feature spotlight

Sensitivity Labels — Automatic protection

When the document is created or saved, the sensitivity label is automatically applied based on your organization's policies. The content is classified and protected from the moment it exists — no manual step required.

Step 2 — Show the sensitivity label

Point out the label applied to the new document

Show the audience that the sensitivity label was automatically applied to the newly created document. This means the content is protected, and sharing/access controls are enforced from the start.

What to expect

The report is formatted for leadership (interpretation + data), the dashboards are included, and the sensitivity label is visible in the document header — showing that enterprise security is baked into the AI workflow, not bolted on after.

Key message for the audience

Two critical points in one scene: (1) Copilot produces leadership-ready output — not just data, but interpretation and recommendations. (2) Security travels with the content — sensitivity labels are applied automatically. Your governance posture is preserved even when AI accelerates the work.

Presenter note

The report generation shows the "Customize Copilot" paying off — the output should already be formatted with green/red, icons, and interpretive prose because of your earlier instructions. Then point to the sensitivity label in the ribbon/header. Don't dwell — just say "notice the label was applied automatically. Security travels with the content." 2-3 minutes.

Accept the meeting and walk in fully prepared — one click.

The contract review meeting is on the calendar. You confirm attendance and ask Copilot to prepare you — pulling together everything relevant from emails, chats, and files.

The report is done. The meeting to discuss contracts is coming up. You go to your calendar, confirm your attendance, and do one last thing: let Copilot prep you so you walk in ready.

Step 1 — Confirm attendance

Accept the meeting invitation

Navigate to your calendar, find the contract review meeting, and confirm your attendance.

Step 2 — Prep for the meeting

Use "Prep me for this meeting"

Click on the meeting and select "Prep me for this meeting" — Copilot pulls together all relevant context: who's attending, what's been discussed in related emails and chats, what files have been shared, and what you should be ready to discuss.

Or manually in Copilot Chat
Prep me for my upcoming contract review meeting. Who's attending? What have we discussed in related emails and chats? What files should I review? What should I be ready to talk about?
What to expect

Copilot assembles: attendee list with roles, recent communications about this topic, relevant files (including the report you just built), and key discussion points. You walk into the meeting fully briefed.

Key message for the audience

Full circle: you started the day catching up on a chat, analyzed the data, built the report, and now you're walking into the decision meeting fully prepared — all in the same morning. No scrambling. No "let me look that up." Ready.

Presenter note

This is the narrative payoff — the audience sees the full arc from notification to meeting-ready in one continuous flow. The "Prep me for this meeting" button is quick to show. Keep it to 1-2 minutes. Then transition: "Now let's look at what happens IN the meeting…"

Scene 7 · Teams · Meeting recap & Facilitator

The meeting ends — and everything that was decided is already captured.

Teams Recap gives you a structured summary. Facilitator takes live notes and creates tasks during the meeting — no note-taker needed.

This is the second scenario. After the meeting, you show how Teams Copilot captures everything — and how the Facilitator agent turns discussion into structured notes and actionable tasks in real time.

Feature spotlight

Teams Recap

After any meeting with transcription enabled, Copilot generates a structured recap — decisions, action items, key topics — available in the meeting chat. Not a transcript. An actionable summary.

Show — Teams Recap

Demonstrate the post-meeting recap

Open a completed meeting and show the Copilot recap: decisions made, action items assigned, key discussion points. Emphasize that this appears automatically — no manual notes needed.

Custom recap format
Give me a recap of this meeting organized as: (1) Decisions made — specific commitments, not themes; (2) Action items — owner, next step, due date; (3) Key discussion points; (4) Open questions we need to follow up on.
Key message for the audience

Nobody needs to be "the note-taker" anymore. The recap captures what matters — decisions and ownership — and it's available to everyone in the meeting chat immediately. No more "can someone send the notes?"

Presenter note

Show a real meeting recap if you have one. The custom format prompt is powerful — show that you can ask for exactly the format you want. Then transition to Facilitator: "But what if you want notes captured during the meeting?"

Scene 8 · Teams · Facilitator & Planner integration

Facilitator takes live notes, creates tasks, and syncs with Planner — during the meeting.

While the meeting is happening, Facilitator captures structured notes. And when someone says "let's add a task" — it happens right there, assigned and tracked.

Facilitator is an agent that participates in your meeting — taking structured notes, capturing decisions, and letting you create tasks on the fly that sync directly to Microsoft Planner.

Live notes

Structured capture, in real time

Facilitator takes notes as the meeting progresses — not a raw transcript, but organized observations: topics discussed, decisions, parking lot items.

Notes are editable during the meeting.
Tasks on demand

Create tasks by @mentioning Facilitator

When a task comes up in discussion, just say it: "@Facilitator add a task about 'discuss use cases' and assign that to Dakota." The task is created immediately and syncs to Planner.

No switching apps. No forgetting.
Planner sync

Tasks appear in Planner instantly

Every task Facilitator creates is tracked in Microsoft Planner — assigned, visible, and ready for follow-up. The meeting's commitments don't live in a notes doc; they live in the work management tool.

Show the tasks in Planner after the meeting.
Step 1 — Show Facilitator taking notes

Demonstrate live note-taking during a meeting

Show how Facilitator captures structured notes as the meeting progresses. Point out that notes are organized by topic, not chronological transcript.

Step 2 — Create a task via @Facilitator

Add a task to Planner right from the meeting

During the meeting, type the @Facilitator mention to create a task on the spot.

Create a task in the meeting
@Facilitator add a task about "discuss use cases" and assign that to Dakota
Step 3 — Show the task in Planner

Navigate to Planner and show the task

After creating the task, switch to Microsoft Planner and show that the task appeared — assigned to Dakota, with the meeting context attached. The loop is closed.

What to expect

The task appears in Planner immediately — assigned to Dakota, tagged from the meeting. No manual entry, no "I'll add that later," no tasks lost between the meeting and the follow-up.

Key message for the audience

Meetings produce three things: decisions, notes, and tasks. Facilitator captures all three without a designated note-taker. And because tasks sync to Planner, commitments don't die in a meeting notes document nobody re-reads. They become real work, tracked and assigned.

Presenter note

The @Facilitator task creation is the wow moment — type it, watch the task appear, then switch to Planner and show it there. Quick, visual, satisfying. If Facilitator isn't available in your environment, narrate it with the concept: "imagine every time someone says 'let's track that,' it actually gets tracked — assigned, in Planner, zero effort." 3 minutes for this whole section.
