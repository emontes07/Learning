Reference

Welcome

This guide is yours to keep. Use it during the workshop to follow along and copy prompts, and come back afterwards to practice on your own. Every prompt has a Copy button, so you never have to retype anything.

We are not here to turn accountants into AI experts. We are here to remove work you should not have to do manually.

What you will do

You will step into the role of a controller at Zava Supply Co., a fictitious distribution company. September is closed, the Q3 forecast update is in, and leadership wants the story behind the numbers. Using Copilot in Excel, you will:

  1. Get a quick win: ask a workbook what matters most, in one sentence.
  2. Work smarter in Excel: find the biggest cost changes, understand what drives them, and build formulas, PivotTables and charts.
  3. Tell the forecast story: explain the move from the prior forecast to the current forecast in a leadership-ready narrative.
  4. Find the actual vs budget drivers: run the September review yourself, including customers and unusual expenses.
  5. Make it repeatable: turn your best prompts into a Monthly Controller Review skill and run it on August.
  6. Plan your month-end: choose one real task you will try with Copilot next cycle.

Learning objectives

  • Choose between Chat, Plan and Edit in Copilot in Excel.
  • Ask useful questions of a finance workbook without advanced formulas.
  • Identify material changes, variances and anomalies that need attention.
  • Draft management commentary and refine it for an executive audience.
  • Recognize monthly tasks that can be standardized with a reusable skill.
  • Leave with one realistic finance task to improve next reporting cycle.

2. Put the files where Copilot can work with them

  1. Upload the files to your OneDrive (for example a folder called Copilot Workshop).
  2. Open them in Excel for the web (type excel.new in your browser, or open from OneDrive). Make sure AutoSave is on.
  3. Select Copilot on the Home ribbon to open the Copilot pane on the right.
Why the web version today?
Copilot features roll out to Excel for the web first. Your desktop Excel follows your company's update schedule, so some options, such as Plugins and skills, may appear there a little later. Everything in this guide works in Excel for the web.

3. Get to know the Copilot pane

Chat

Ask questions and get answers, explanations and suggestions. Your workbook is not changed. Best for understanding data and drafting text.

Plan

Copilot proposes the steps it would take before doing anything. You review and approve. Best for multi-step work you want to check first.

Edit

Shown as Allow editing. Copilot makes changes in the workbook: columns, formulas, PivotTables, charts, formatting. Undo with Ctrl+Z.

Look for the mode selector under the chat box. Other things you may see: a + button (add files, plugins and skills), a model picker (the default is fine), and the ... menu at the top of the pane.

4. Ground rules

You own the output
Copilot gets you to a strong first draft faster. You are still accountable for every number and every word that leaves your desk. You can outsource the processing, but not the responsibility.
Your data stays protected
When you are signed in with your work account, Microsoft 365 Copilot works within your company's security and compliance boundary and your prompts are not used to train the models. Always follow Bunzl's data policies.
  • Verify numbers. Ask Copilot where a number came from, then check the cell.
  • Save a copy first (File > Save a copy) so you can return to the original. Ctrl+Z undoes Copilot edits.
  • Different answers are normal. Your neighbor may get different wording. Compare and keep the best.
  • Context beats clever wording. You do not need perfect prompts. State the outcome, the data, and the format you want.

Zava Supply Co. is a fictitious distributor of everyday essentials with four business units: Foodservice Packaging, Janitorial & Sanitation, Safety & PPE and Retail Supplies. The reports in your files follow a familiar layout: Sales, Gross Margin, GM %, Op Costs, Op Cost %, PBITA, PBITA % and Sales/Day, with Actual, Budget and Prior Year for the month and year to date.

TuesdayThe Q3 forecast update (9+3) replaced the Q2 forecast (6+6).
FridayLeadership monthly business review: they want to know why, not just what.

Last month you spent most of a day copying numbers into slides and writing commentary from scratch. This month you will let Copilot do the heavy lifting, while you bring the judgment.

Tip: the files contain a few things worth finding, such as a new customer, a lost customer, cost pressure and a couple of unusual postings. Let Copilot help you find them.

2.2Ask what is driving the changes
Time 7 minGoal Separate one-time from run-rateUse Sep file > OpEx_Detail, Customer SalesLevel Beginner

A ranked list is a start. A controller wants to know is this a blip or the new normal?

Try this promptChat mode
For the top five unfavorable items from your last answer, look at January to September 2026 in OpEx_Detail and tell me whether each one is a one-time spike, a new run-rate, or a possible timing or posting issue. Explain your reasoning in one sentence each and mention the months you looked at.
Follow-up promptChat mode
Which customer or event in this workbook could explain the Foodservice Packaging overtime and temporary labor increase that starts in July? Check the Customer Sales sheet.
What to expect
  • Bad Debt (Safety & PPE): one-time spike in September.
  • Overtime and Temporary Labor (Foodservice Packaging): a new run-rate since July, three months in a row.
  • Marketing (Retail Supplies): new in September.
  • Copilot should connect the July step-up to Harbor Grill Group, a new customer with sales from July and no prior-year sales.
Correlation is not explanation
Copilot can spot that Harbor Grill Group and the overtime both start in July. Only you, or the business, can confirm that onboarding the new customer caused it. Treat these as leads to follow up, not conclusions.

2.3Let Copilot build it: a column, a PivotTable and a chart
Time 8 minGoal Make changes in the workbookUse Sep file > OpEx_DetailLevel Beginner

Switch to Edit mode (Allow editing). Copilot will now change the workbook. Run these one at a time and look at the result before moving on.

Build a columnEdit mode
Add a column to the OpEx_Detail table called 'Fav/Unfav vs Budget' that shows 'Unfavorable' when Var vs Budget $ is greater than zero and 'Favorable' otherwise.
Build a PivotTableEdit mode

Build a chartEdit mode

What to expect
A new calculated column in the table, a PivotTable on its own sheet, and a chart where the overtime and temporary labor lines jump in July while the budget lines stay flat.
Try Plan mode
Not sure what Copilot will do? Switch to Plan and paste the PivotTable prompt. Copilot lists its steps first and you approve them. Great for bigger changes.
Undo is your friend
If Copilot did something you did not want, press Ctrl+Z or tell it: "Undo the last change."

2.4Use Copilot as your Excel coach
Time 4 minGoal Understand an existing formulaUse Sep file > GL P&L Detail, SummaryLevel Beginner

Inherited workbooks are full of formulas nobody remembers writing. Ask Copilot to explain them. Switch back to Chat.

Try this promptChat mode
Explain the formula in cell F7 on the GL P&L Detail sheet in plain language. Why does it use ABS and IFERROR?
Follow-up promptChat mode
On the Summary sheet, how is Sales/Day calculated, and why would a controller look at Sales/Day instead of total Sales when comparing months?
What to expect
  • F7 is the Act vs Bud Var %: (Actual - Budget) / |Budget|. ABS keeps the sign meaningful when the budget is negative (for example returns or rebates). IFERROR shows 0 instead of an error when the budget is zero.
  • Sales/Day = Sales / billing days. It removes the effect of months with more or fewer working days (September 2026 has 21 billing days, October has 22).
Stretch
Ask: "Write a formula for a new column on the Summary sheet that shows 'Review' when the absolute Act vs Bud Var % is more than 10%." Then ask Copilot to explain it back to you.

3.3Draft the management narrative, with your color commentary
Time 8 minGoal A leadership-ready first draftUse Forecast file > Overview, AdjustmentsLevel Intermediate

The workbook knows the numbers. You know things that are not in the workbook. Add that context to the prompt; this is what turns a generic summary into your story. Edit the text in square brackets with anything you would actually say.

Try this promptChat mode
Draft a management-ready narrative explaining the change from the Prior FCAST (Q2 6+6) to the Current FCAST (Q3 9+3) for full-year 2026. Use short sections for Sales, Gross Margin, Op Costs and PBITA. Lead with the consolidated PBITA change, then explain the largest drivers by business unit using the Adjustments sheet. Show amounts in $ millions with one decimal. Include this context that is not in the workbook: [Harbor Grill Group onboarding overtime is expected to end in November. Nitrile price increase letters went to customers on October 15 with a November 1 effective date.] Keep it under 250 words, factual and professional.
What to expect
A structured narrative that opens with PBITA down about $3.3M (-9.8%) to about $30.5M, explains that sales are up $8.1M mainly from Harbor Grill Group, and that margin pressure in Safety & PPE (nitrile costs), new-customer onboarding costs, one-time items and the Ridgeline Fabrication reserve more than offset it. Your color commentary should appear in the right sections.
Your judgment is the value
Read every sentence. Is the emphasis right? Is anything missing that leadership will ask about? Would you be comfortable presenting this? If not, tell Copilot what to change, for example: "Make Safety & PPE the lead story" or "Remove the word significant."

Module 5

From Prompt to Repeatable Process

20 minutesSkills, see one and do one
The story

You just built a great September review. Next month you would have to remember every prompt and every rule again. Instead, capture the recipe once as a skill, a natural-language macro, and run it every month-end. You do not need to be technical: describe what you want, and let Copilot do most of the writing.

5.1Write down your recipe
Time 3 minGoal Know what 'good' looks likeUse Your notes from Modules 3 and 4Level Intermediate

Look back at what you just did. A strong monthly controller review followed these steps:

  1. Identify variances
  2. Separate key drivers
  3. Highlight anomalies
  4. Flag follow-up items
  5. Draft commentary
  6. Format an executive summary

Also note the rules you had to repeat: the sign convention for costs, using prior year as the customer budget proxy, and "say so if the data doesn't explain it". Those rules belong in the skill.

Self-check
5.2Create the Monthly Controller Review skill
Time 6 minGoal Capture the recipe onceUse Excel for the web, Copilot paneLevel Intermediate

Option A: let Copilot write it. In the Sep file, ask:

Try this promptChat mode
Based on the analysis we just did, write reusable instructions for a skill called 'Monthly Controller Review' that I can run every month on a workbook with the same structure (Summary, GL P&L Detail, OpEx_Detail, Customer Sales). Include the purpose, inputs, rules (sign convention, materiality threshold, how to treat customer budget), step-by-step instructions, the output format, and what it must not do.

Option B: start from ours. Copy this ready-made version and adjust it to your taste.

Monthly Controller Review skill instructions
Monthly Controller Review

Purpose: Produce a consistent monthly controller review for a monthly results workbook with sheets named Summary, GL P&L Detail, OpEx_Detail and Customer Sales. Amounts are in USD thousands.

Inputs: the month to review (for example "August 2026"). If not given, use the latest month in the Summary sheet.

Rules:
- For Sales, Gross Margin and PBITA a positive variance is favorable. For Cost of Sales and Op Costs a positive variance is unfavorable.
- Material = absolute variance of at least $50K and at least 5%.
- Budget is prior year plus growth; there is no customer budget. Use change vs prior year by customer as the proxy for budget drivers.
- Only state reasons that are supported by the workbook. If the reason is not in the data, write "Reason not in data - confirm with business unit".
- Do not change any numbers in the workbook.

Steps:
1. Variances: list material variances vs budget and vs prior year for Sales, Gross Margin, GM %, Op Costs and PBITA, consolidated and by business unit.
2. Key drivers: for each business unit, separate drivers into Sales (customers from Customer Sales), Gross Margin and Op Costs (accounts from OpEx_Detail).
3. Anomalies: from OpEx_Detail, flag any account-month more than 50% above budget or prior year and classify it as one-time, possible posting error, or new run-rate.
4. Follow-up: list open questions with a suggested owner (business unit controller, operations, credit, purchasing).
5. Commentary: draft Sales, Gross Margin, Op Costs and PBITA commentary in $000s with variance vs budget and vs prior year.
6. Executive summary: three bullets, maximum 25 words each.

Output format:
- Section headings in this order: Headline, Variances table, Drivers by business unit, Anomalies, Follow-ups, Commentary, Executive summary.
- End with a "Numbers to verify" list showing each figure used and the sheet it came from.

Save it as a skill. In the Copilot pane, select + in the chat box and look for Plugins and skills. If you can create a custom skill, name it Monthly Controller Review and paste the instructions.

Don't see the option?
Menus are evolving and may differ by version, which is one reason we are using Excel for the web today. If skills are not available to you yet, save the instructions in a Word document or OneNote page and paste them at the start of your chat. You get the same consistent result.
Self-check

Troubleshooting

I don't see the Copilot button

Make sure the file is saved in OneDrive or SharePoint and AutoSave is on. Try Excel for the web (excel.new) and make sure you are signed in with your work account.

I don't see Plugins and skills in desktop Excel

Desktop Excel updates on your company's schedule, so newer options can appear in Excel for the web first. Use Excel for the web, or use the instructions-in-a-document fallback from Lab 5.2.

Copilot says it can't find the data

Name the sheet and the columns in your prompt. Copilot works best with data formatted as an Excel table (Home > Format as Table). Most sheets in the workshop files are already tables.

My answer is different from my neighbor's

That is normal. Generative AI can phrase and organize answers differently each time. Compare the substance, check the numbers, and keep the better prompt.

Copilot changed something I didn't want

Press Ctrl+Z, or ask Copilot to undo the last change. Use Plan mode when you want to approve steps first.

Glossary

PBITAProfit before interest, tax and amortization. Here: Gross Margin minus Op Costs.
GM %Gross Margin as a percentage of Sales.
Op Costs / Op Cost %Warehouse & Delivery + Selling + G&A, and that total as a percentage of Sales.
Sales/DaySales divided by billing days, so months of different lengths compare fairly.
FCAST 6+6 / 9+36 months actual + 6 months forecast (Q2 update); 9 actual + 3 forecast (Q3 update).
Operational AdjustmentsThe items that move the prior forecast to the current forecast.
R&OsRisks and opportunities not yet included in the forecast.
ptsPercentage points: the difference between two percentages.
