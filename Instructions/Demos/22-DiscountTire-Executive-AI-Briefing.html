Opening

We're not walking through features today — we're walking through how your day changes with AI.

Discount Tire has built the largest independent tire dealership in the country by doing the fundamentals better than anyone else — relentless customer service, smart inventory, and a culture of execution. But the world those stores operate in is changing. Customers compare prices on their phone while standing in your lobby. Supply chains shift faster than quarterly reviews can track. That's what Microsoft 365 Copilot is. Not a new app to learn. Not another tool on the taskbar. It's the M365 you already have — Excel, Word, PowerPoint, Teams, Outlook — working fundamentally differently. AI woven into every workflow you already run.

1
7:00 AM · Start the day · Copilot Chat

Catch up, prioritize, and walk into the day already ahead.

A leader opens their laptop with a coffee. Ten minutes later they're oriented, prioritized, and ready for their first meeting — without scrolling through 60 emails.

The old pattern: open Outlook, scroll, react, get lost in threads from three time zones. The new pattern: ask Copilot what matters, get a briefing in 30 seconds, and start the day on offense.

Copilot Chat

The morning briefing

One question replaces 30 minutes of inbox scanning. Copilot reads across your email, chats, and calendar to surface what actually needs attention — not everything, just what matters.

Works from your phone, your browser, or Teams.
Outlook

Inbox prioritized by action

Copilot in Outlook summarizes long email threads, drafts replies in your voice, and highlights what needs a decision vs. what's just FYI. Three replies sent before 7:30.

Your voice, your tone. Not a robot.
Teams

Catch up on chats you missed

"Catch me up on this chat" — Copilot reads the thread and gives you the summary with key decisions and action items, so you don't have to scroll through 200 messages.

Every Teams chat, every channel.
Try this in Copilot Chat (BizChat)
What should I focus on today? Summarize my most important emails from the last 24 hours, highlight any messages that need a decision from me, and list my meetings for today with any preparation I should do.
Try this in Outlook
Summarize this email thread. What was decided? What's still open? Draft a reply confirming the next steps and asking for the timeline on the outstanding items.
Try this in Teams
Catch me up on this conversation. What were the key decisions? Are there any action items assigned to me?
Outcome by 7:30 AM

The leader arrives at their first meeting already current. Three replies sent. Meeting prep done. The day isn't running them — they're running it.

Presenter note

This section lands fast. Show BizChat live — use your own inbox data. The "what should I focus on today" prompt is the crowd-pleaser. If time is tight, skip the Outlook and Teams prompts and reference them as "the same intelligence, everywhere you work."

2
9:00 AM · Turn data into action · Excel

From spreadsheet to strategy in minutes — not days.

The data is there. It's always been there. The bottleneck was never the data — it was the time it took to turn it into something a leader could act on.

Every week, someone on the team spends hours formatting data, building pivot tables, and creating charts so leadership can see what's happening. Copilot in Excel changes that equation: describe what you want to see, and the analysis builds itself. The person who used to format spreadsheets now spends that time interpreting what the data means.

Analyze

Ask questions in plain English

"What are my top 10 stores by revenue this quarter?" — Copilot reads the data, does the math, and gives you the answer. No formulas to write. No pivot table to build.

Works with any table-formatted data.
Visualize

Charts and insights in seconds

"Show me a trend chart of monthly revenue by region" — Copilot creates the chart, picks the right format, and you refine from there. From raw data to visual insight in one prompt.

Recommended charts based on your data.
Transform

Clean, sort, and format

"Add a column that categorizes each store as High, Medium, or Low based on their revenue vs. target percentage" — Copilot writes the formula and applies it. Business logic in plain language.

Complex formulas without the syntax.
Try this in Excel — Analyze
What are the top 10 performing locations by total revenue? Show me a breakdown by category and highlight any that are trending down compared to last quarter.
Try this in Excel — Visualize
Create a chart showing monthly revenue trends by region for the last 12 months. Add a trend line and highlight any months where revenue dropped more than 10% from the prior month.
Try this in Excel — Advanced formula
Add a new column called 'Performance Rating'. Categorize each row as 'Exceeding' if actual is more than 110% of target, 'On Track' if between 90-110%, and 'Needs Attention' if below 90%. Then sort the entire table by this rating with 'Needs Attention' first.
Tip

Always verify the numbers. Copilot is powerful, but it's an assistant — not an auditor. Spot-check formulas and totals before sharing data with stakeholders. The AI does the heavy lifting; you own the final answer.

Outcome by 9:45 AM

A data set that used to require an hour of formatting and formula work is now analyzed, visualized, and ready for a conversation. The leader isn't waiting for someone to "pull the numbers" — the numbers are already pulled, charted, and sorted by what matters most.

Presenter note

If you have a sample dataset, open it and do the analysis live. The "top 10" prompt is fast and visual. If you don't have data ready, narrate the workflow and show the Copilot pane. Emphasize the shift: the person who used to build the spreadsheet now interprets it.

3
10:30 AM · Build the message · Word & PowerPoint

The hardest part of any deliverable is starting it. That problem is gone.

The blank page is the enemy of progress. Whether it's a report, a proposal, or a deck for leadership — Copilot gives you a running start so you can focus on making it yours.

Two scenarios every team faces weekly: someone needs to write something and someone needs to present something. In both cases, the pain isn't the final product — it's the first 80% of effort to get to a draft worth editing. Copilot handles that 80%.

Word — Write with confidence

Draft

From blank page to first draft

"Write a quarterly business review summary covering our performance against targets, key wins, challenges, and recommended next steps." Copilot writes the structure and prose. You edit the substance.

Transform

Rewrite, adjust tone, condense

Select any paragraph and ask Copilot to make it more concise, adjust the tone for a different audience, or expand a bullet point into a full section. The same content, tuned for the reader.

Try this in Word — Draft a document
Write a quarterly business review document. Include sections for: Executive Summary, Performance vs. Targets (with placeholder for key metrics), Top 3 Wins This Quarter, Challenges and Risks, and Recommended Actions for Next Quarter. Use a professional but approachable tone. Keep it to 2 pages.
Try this in Word — Rewrite selected text
Rewrite this section to be more concise and action-oriented. Keep the key points but cut the length in half. Make the tone confident — this is going to senior leadership.

PowerPoint — Present with impact

Create

Deck from scratch or from a document

"Create a presentation from this document" — Copilot reads your Word doc, structures the narrative into slides, and applies a clean design. You had a report; now you have a deck.

Enhance

Add slides, speaker notes, polish

"Add a slide summarizing the key risks" or "Write speaker notes for each slide" — Copilot extends and polishes the deck without you rebuilding from scratch.

Try this in PowerPoint — Create a deck
Create a presentation about our team's quarterly performance. Include slides for: Title, Agenda, Key Metrics Dashboard, Top Wins, Challenges We're Addressing, Action Plan for Next Quarter, and a Thank You / Q&A slide. Use a clean, modern design. Keep it to 8-10 slides.
Try this in PowerPoint — Add to existing deck
Add a slide after this one that summarizes the top 3 risks we're watching this quarter. Use a visual layout with icons or callout boxes. Keep the text concise — no more than 3 bullet points per risk.
Try this in PowerPoint — Speaker notes
Write speaker notes for each slide in this presentation. The notes should help the presenter tell a story — not just read the slide. Include transition phrases between slides and key talking points to emphasize.
Outcome by 11:15 AM

A report that would have taken the morning is drafted in 10 minutes and polished in 15. A deck that would have taken the afternoon is structured, designed, and has speaker notes. The leader's energy goes to refining the message, not building the container.

Presenter note

The Word → PowerPoint flow is compelling: draft in Word, then "Create a presentation from this document" in PowerPoint. It shows the tools working together. If short on time, pick one — the Word draft prompt is fastest, the PowerPoint "from document" is most impressive. Emphasize that Copilot gives you a first draft you'd be willing to edit, not a finished product.

4
1:00 PM · Close the loop · Teams Meetings

Meetings finally do what they were supposed to — drive decisions and action.

The problem was never the meeting itself. The problem was everything that happened (or didn't happen) after. Copilot changes the after.

Think about your last important meeting. How long before the follow-up went out? Did everyone remember what was decided? Who owned what? In most organizations, the meeting ends and 40% of what was agreed starts fading within the hour. Copilot in Teams fixes that — not by recording more, but by capturing what matters and making it actionable.

During

Real-time intelligence

Copilot listens during the meeting and can answer questions in real time: "What has been discussed so far?" or "What action items have been mentioned?" — without interrupting the flow.

Private to you. Others can't see your prompts.
After

Custom recap, not a transcript

Ask for a recap in the format you want: decisions, owners, due dates, open questions. Not a 40-page transcript — a 1-page summary of what actually matters.

Available in the Teams meeting chat.
Catch-up

Missed the meeting? Ask Copilot.

"What was decided in the ops review I missed?" — Copilot reads the transcript and gives you the summary. You're caught up in 60 seconds, not 60 minutes of recording playback.

Respects permissions. Only meetings you're invited to.
Try this during a Teams meeting
What action items have been mentioned so far? List each one with the person who was assigned to it.
Try this after a meeting — Custom recap
Give me a recap of this meeting organized as: (1) Decisions made — specific commitments, not themes; (2) Action items — owner, next step, and due date; (3) Key discussion points worth remembering; (4) Open questions still unresolved.
Try this after a meeting — Follow-up email
Draft a follow-up email based on this meeting. Include the key decisions, action items with owners, and next steps. Use a professional but warm tone. Address it to the meeting attendees.
Try this to catch up on a meeting you missed
Summarize what I missed in this meeting. What were the main topics? Were any decisions made? Are there action items that involve me or my team?
Outcome by 1:45 PM

The meeting ends and the follow-up is already sent. Every owner knows what they own. The leader who missed the first 10 minutes is caught up. Nothing decided today dies in a forgotten thread next week.

Presenter note

The custom recap prompt is the star here — it's what the SSP specifically requested and what VPs care about most. If you can, show it from a real meeting that just happened (or use a recent meeting transcript). The "draft a follow-up email" prompt shows the Teams → Outlook handoff. This is where execs say "I need this today."

5
3:00 PM · Scale with Agents · The next chapter

Everything so far helps you work faster. Agents help you scale.

Copilot makes one person more productive. Agents make a process run without waiting for a person at every step.

There's a difference between making people faster and making processes smarter. Everything we've shown today — the inbox briefing, the Excel analysis, the Word draft, the meeting recap — makes a person more effective. Agents go further: they take a repeatable workflow and let it run on its own, with the right people involved at the right moments.

"The question isn't 'how do I use AI?' It's 'which processes in my organization are still waiting on a person to do something a machine should handle?'"
Copilot Studio

Build agents — no code required

Copilot Studio lets anyone in the organization build an AI agent that can answer questions, look up data, trigger workflows, and connect to your systems. Describe what it should do; it does the rest.

Point it at your knowledge. It learns your domain.
SharePoint agents

An expert on your content

Create an agent grounded in a specific SharePoint site — like your HR policy library, your store operations manual, or your safety procedures. Employees ask questions; the agent answers from your approved content.

Answers cite the source document.
Agents in Teams

Agents where people already work

Deploy agents directly into Teams channels and chats. A new hire asks "What's the PTO policy?" in a Teams channel — the agent answers immediately, accurately, and cites the source. No ticket. No wait.

Available 24/7. Consistent answers every time.

Real-world agent examples

HR & Policy

Employee self-service

"What's the dress code for store employees?" "How do I request a transfer?" "What's the holiday schedule?" — an agent grounded on your HR documents answers instantly, 24/7, and always cites the policy source.

Store Operations

Procedures & troubleshooting

"What's the process for a warranty claim?" "How do I handle a customer escalation?" "What's the closing checklist?" — a store ops agent gives consistent, accurate answers grounded in your SOPs.

What you'd tell Copilot Studio
Create an agent that answers employee questions about company policies. It should be grounded on our SharePoint HR site. It should always cite the specific document and section it's referencing. If it doesn't know the answer, it should say 'I don't have that information — please contact HR directly.'
What this means

Instead of 50 people asking the same question and waiting for someone to respond, one agent gives the right answer — instantly, consistently, and sourced. The people who used to field those questions get their time back for higher-value work.

Presenter note

Agents are the "wow" moment for many VPs — the idea that their team can build these without IT or code is powerful. If time is tight, focus on the SharePoint agent concept — it's the easiest to explain and most immediately relatable ("imagine your store ops manual could answer questions"). Skip the Copilot Studio prompt if time is short; it's there for the curious.

6 · Trust & governance

The questions every leader should ask — and the short answers.

The story above only works if leaders trust the system underneath it. Four principles, kept deliberately short.

🔒

Permission-based

Copilot respects your existing Microsoft 365 access controls. If someone can't see a file today, Copilot can't see it for them either. No new permissions to manage.

👁

Your data stays yours

Your business data is not used to train Microsoft's AI models. Copilot works within your tenant, your boundaries, your rules. Period.

✋

Human in the loop

Copilot suggests. People decide. Every email, every document, every action goes through a human before it's final. AI is the assistant; your people are in charge.

🧭

Enterprise-grade security

Built on the same security, compliance, and privacy infrastructure that protects your Microsoft 365 data today. SOC 2, ISO 27001, and your existing compliance posture — all intact.

"The leaders who adopt AI fastest are the ones who can answer 'is this safe?' in one sentence. We've made that sentence short on purpose."
Presenter note

Don't spend more than 60 seconds here unless there are questions. The four tiles say it all. If someone asks a deep governance question, offer a follow-up session with their IT team — that's the right forum, not a 20-minute exec briefing.

7 · What's next

What should the next 90 days look like?

Not a roadmap. A starting move. The leaders in this room can set each of these in motion this week.

Step 1

Pick two daily habits

The two things leaders in this room do every day that AI can make faster: morning inbox triage, meeting prep, data review, follow-up emails. Start there.

Step 2

Identify two team workflows

The two repeatable processes your teams run every week that could be faster with Copilot — or automated with an agent. These become the first wins.

Step 3

Name champions

One champion per workflow — a person, not a committee. They learn the tool, build the prompts, share what works, and coach the team.

Step 4

Review in 90 days

Four habits. Four champions. Ninety days. Then a review with this room — what worked, what didn't, and what scales next across the organization.

The leaders who change how Discount Tire operates won't be the ones who learn every AI feature. They'll be the ones who pick four things, name four owners, and protect ninety days. Everything else follows.
